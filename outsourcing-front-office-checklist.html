Before you outsource front-office work: a practice manager's checklist

Remote admin staff can take real weight off a front office. The practices that get the most from it settle a handful of questions before the first login.

1. Which tasks move, and which stay

Good candidates are high-volume, rule-based and done on a screen: eligibility checks, prior auth follow-up, referral processing, portal messages, charge entry and denial work. Keep in-person tasks and anything needing clinical judgment in the building.

2. How access works

  • Does the vendor work inside your EMR and portals, or copy data into their own tools?
  • Does each person get a named login that you issue and can revoke?
  • Are printing, downloads and local storage blocked on their workstations?

3. The paperwork

Get a signed Business Associate Agreement before any access. Read how it handles breach notification and subcontractors. Ask to see the vendor's written security policies, not a summary.

4. Training on your way of working

Generic experience helps, but your note formats, payer quirks and escalation rules are specific. Ask how the vendor captures them, and whether you'll get a written playbook you can keep.

5. Who oversees the work

Name one internal owner. Agree a daily or weekly check-in, a way to flag errors, and who on the vendor side fixes them.

6. How you'll know it's working

Pick two or three measures before you start and record the baseline: for example, eligibility denial rate, days to auth decision, open referrals older than 14 days, or A/R over 90 days. [STAT: record your baseline for each measure you choose]

7. The exit

Know how access is removed, how work in progress is handed back and how much notice either side needs. A good vendor will be comfortable answering this on the first call.
